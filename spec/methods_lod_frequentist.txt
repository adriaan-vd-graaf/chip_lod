Frequentist variant calling and limit of detection. For a site sequenced to depth n with k reads supporting the alternate allele, the number of alternate reads produced by sequencing error alone was modelled as Poisson with mean λ0 = n·e/3, where e is the per-base error rate and the factor 1/3 reflects that only errors to the specific alternate base contribute. The one-sided p-value of an observed count is P(K ≥ k | λ0), evaluated as an explicit upper-tail sum of Poisson probabilities computed in log space. The critical value k*(n) is the smallest integer with P(K ≥ k* | λ0) ≤ α, and a site is called when k ≥ k*(n). For a heterozygous variant at variant allele fraction (VAF) p, the expected fraction of alternate reads is r(p) = p(1 − e) + (1 − p)·e/3, so the alternate-read count is Poisson with mean n·r(p) and the probability of a call (power) is P(K ≥ k*(n) | n·r(p)). The limit of detection (LoD) at depth n is the smallest VAF in [0, 0.5] whose power is at least 1 − β, obtained by bisection (100 iterations, upper bracket reported); it is undefined if even p = 0.5 does not reach this power. Because k* is an integer, the realised false-positive rate P(K ≥ k* | λ0) is at most α and usually below it, and the LoD is a saw-tooth rather than a strictly decreasing function of depth. We used α = 0.05 and 1 − β = 0.95. The function lod_frequentist(n, k, e, α, 1 − β), implemented in Python and R with byte-identical output, takes vectors of depths and alternate-read counts and returns λ0, the p-value, k*, the call and the LoD for every site, computing k* and the LoD once per distinct depth.

Limit of detection. Sequencing errors can produce reads that look like a variant, so we first estimated how many
such reads to expect at a site without a variant. Only about one in three base-calling errors produces the specific
variant base, so a site sequenced to depth n with a per-base error rate e is expected to carry n·e/3 error reads,
and we modelled their number with a Poisson distribution. From this we derived, for each depth, the read-count limit
of detection k*: the smallest number of variant reads that errors alone produce in fewer than 5% of variant-free
samples. A variant was called when it was supported by at least k* reads. A real heterozygous variant at variant
allele fraction (VAF) p contributes variant reads at a rate of p(1 − e) + (1 − p)·e/3, close to p, which tells us
how often it reaches k* reads. The VAF limit of detection is the lowest VAF that reaches k* reads in at least 95% of
samples. Because read counts are whole numbers, the false-positive rate is at most 5% and the limit of detection does
not decrease smoothly with depth. All values were computed with the function lod_frequentist (Python and R).
